# Wealth Personas

> **Source:** `Wealth Personas.pptx`. Three personas define who the new Wealth experience (and the AI features) are built for. Use these to tune tone, defaults, and which use case matters most.

Persona → tier mapping is approximate (personas are asset-band based, matching the tier thresholds).

## 1. Elena the Emerging Investor

**Investable assets:** $0–$100K (~ Everyday Wealth)

Early career, balancing competing financial priorities. Limited investing experience. High sensitivity to fees and risk. Often dealing with debt or cash-flow constraints.

**Pain points**

- Low financial confidence — unsure how to start or which products fit.
- Competing priorities — student loans, rent, childcare, cost of living.
- Fear of losing money — inexperience makes her risk-averse.
- Feels "too small to matter" to advisors/institutions.
- Limited disposable income — hard to contribute regularly.

**Motivators**

- Building financial stability — emergency fund, debt reduction, first investments.
- Simple, low-effort guidance — automation, easy onboarding, low complexity.
- Trust and reassurance — education-first, approachable experts.
- Low fees and transparency.

**AI implications**

- **UC1 (Summary):** reassurance + plain-language "where you stand"; celebrate small progress.
- **UC3 (Spending):** highest relevance — cash-flow/debt-aware nudges, "found money" to start investing.
- **UC2 (Future):** motivate by showing that small, regular contributions compound.

## 2. Elijah the Empowered Switcher

**Investable assets:** $100K–$500K (~ Preferred Wealth)

Mid-career individual/family with increasing stability. Higher confidence but still juggling milestones. Underserved by weak advice and fragmented experiences.

**Pain points**

- Weak/generic advice; not seeing meaningful guidance or returns.
- Digital frustrations — outdated, clunky tools.
- Fee–performance mismatch.
- Overwhelm + low confidence — doesn't want to research funds/strategies.
- Switching friction — moving institutions feels risky/high-effort.

**Motivators**

- Effortless, hands-off investing that "runs in the background".
- Stability and long-term confidence over high-risk returns.
- A single trusted partner — consolidate accounts + advice.
- Digital-first, guided experience backed by human expertise.
- Fair value and transparency — will switch if experience/performance/fees align.

**AI implications**

- **UC2 (Future):** strong fit — "am I on track?" projections drive consolidation.
- **UC1 (Summary):** a confident, consolidated single-view narrative reduces overwhelm.
- **UC3 (Spending):** surface opportunities to increase contributions.

## 3. Esme the Established Mass Affluent

**Investable assets:** $500K–$1MM+ (~ Ultra / Premier Wealth)

Financially stable, investment-savvy, focused on long-term legacy and retirement readiness. Expects high service and personalized strategy.

**Pain points**

- Market uncertainty — protecting accumulated wealth.
- Complex needs — multiple accounts, tax planning, estate.
- Trust/advisor quality — skeptical of generic advice.
- Transition to retirement — income planning, timing, risk.
- Lack of integration — fragmented tools/advice.

**Motivators**

- Holistic wealth management — tax, retirement income, estate.
- Personalized service — bespoke portfolios, 1:1 advisory.
- Capital preservation + growth — risk-managed strategies.
- Legacy & family planning — intergenerational wealth, giving.
- High-quality digital tools across planning, tracking, communication.

**AI implications**

- **UC1 (Summary):** sophisticated, holistic net-worth + asset-mix narrative; decumulation readiness.
- **UC2 (Future):** retirement income / "money lasts" / legacy-at-90 framing (see FP POC).
- **UC3 (Spending):** less about restraint, more about tax-efficient cash-flow & giving.

## Cross-persona design notes

- Confidence and trust are universal needs → **plain language, transparency, and "why we're saying this"** matter in every AI output.
- Fee sensitivity is high for Elena/Elijah → AI can surface fee/MER awareness.
- All three want a **single, consolidated picture** → net worth aggregation (internal + external) is the backbone all three use cases lean on.
